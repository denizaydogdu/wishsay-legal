WishSay Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

This policy explains what personal data WishSay collects, why, who can see it and what rights you have. It is written to meet the Turkish Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 ("KVKK") and the EU General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR"). Under KVKK, this text also serves as our information notice (aydınlatma metni) under Article 10.

What you write is up to you. Please be careful with sensitive information such as health, religion or belief, or sex life, about yourself or others, and with anything identifying a real person: posts can be seen by every user, and named posts show your handle. The app reminds you of this when you write a post or a comment. Our Content Policy forbids sharing other people's personal information.

Removing information about you. If a post or comment contains your personal or sensitive information, you can ask for it to be removed: report it with the reason "Contains my personal / sensitive information" (these reports are flagged for moderators to handle first), or write to info@velzon.tr. This works whether or not you have an account, and whether you or someone else wrote it.

Language of a text and "Translate": When you write a post or comment, or edit its text, the language it is written in is detected automatically on our own server with a language identification library (Lingua). The text is not sent to any third party or external service for this. If the language cannot be determined reliably (very short text, only emoji, mixed languages) it is stored as "undetermined". It is only used to offer the "Translate" button for the right language and to show texts in languages other than Turkish and English to moderators for review. The language of older posts and comments has been re-detected once in the same way.

Post images. When you upload an image we process it on our own server and never send it to a third party:

  • All hidden metadata is removed that phones add to photos: where the photo was taken (GPS location), date and time, device model, camera settings and similar (EXIF, XMP and others). The image is rebuilt from its pixels only, scaled down so its longest side is at most 2048 pixels, and a small preview is made. The original file you uploaded is not kept. What is visible in the picture (faces, number plates, documents, your house number) is not removed: be careful what you share.
  • Every image is automatically screened for inappropriate content (nudity, sexual content) by an open-source AI model running on our server. The model only produces a probability score; if it reaches a set threshold, the image is not deleted and no automated decision is made about you: it is only visible to you and to moderators until a moderator reviews it. The post itself is still published. The moderator approves or rejects the image; a rejected image is deleted and you are notified. The model can be wrong; a harmless image being held for review does not create a negative record about you.
  • Images are shown to everyone who can see your post through short-lived (15-minute) signed links that contain nothing that identifies you. An image on an anonymous post is anonymous too, but its content may still reveal who you are.
  • Images that are not added to a post within 24 hours of uploading are deleted automatically.

What we don't do: WishSay has no ads, no third-party analytics or tracking tools, and does not send push notifications. We do not sell your data.

Explicit consent for sensitive information

If you share information about yourself such as your health, religion or belief, or sex life in your posts and comments, this is a special category of personal data under KVKK Article 6 (GDPR Article 9). The legal basis for showing it to other users is your explicit consent. When you sign up you give it with a separate checkbox for this text; the box is unchecked at first and you cannot sign up without ticking it:

"I give my explicit consent that sensitive information about myself that I share (such as health, religion or sex life) will be visible to other users."

How it is recorded: each time you give or withdraw this consent, a separate consent record is created: whether it was given or withdrawn, the current version of the Content Policy, the time (UTC), IP address, source port, device/app information (User-Agent) and request ID. These records cannot be changed and are kept like the other consent records (see section 6).

How to withdraw it: at any time, turn off Settings > Sensitive data consent in the app, or write to info@velzon.tr. If you have not given it (for example because you signed up before this feature) or have withdrawn it, the app asks you with the same text when you want to write a post or comment; you can give it again whenever you like.

What withdrawing means: without this consent you cannot write new posts or comments or edit the text of your existing ones. Everything else keeps working, such as reading, liking, reporting, managing tags and deleting your own content. Withdrawing does not remove what you have already posted: your existing posts and comments stay visible. If you want them removed, delete them in the app or write to info@velzon.tr. Withdrawing does not affect the lawfulness of the processing based on your consent before you withdrew it.

This consent only covers information about yourself. Sharing someone else's sensitive information in a way that identifies them is forbidden by the Content Policy.

What the activity records contain

For each of the following actions, the information above (user, action, IP address, source port, User-Agent, request ID and UTC time) is recorded:

  • Registration: your email address, handle, language, settings, the policy versions you accepted and that you gave explicit consent for sensitive information.
  • Giving and withdrawing the explicit consent for sensitive information later on (with the type of consent and the policy version).
  • Logging in, failed login attempts, session refresh and logout. For a failed attempt, the email address that was entered and the reason for the failure are recorded, even if that address does not belong to an account. Passwords are never recorded. Requests refused because of too many attempts in a short time are recorded too (once per IP address or email address).
  • Email verification: that a verification code was sent, and successful and failed verification attempts (the email address entered and the reason; the code itself is never recorded). If someone tries to open a new account with your email address, that attempt and the requested handle are recorded and you get an email about it.
  • Settings changes: the old and new values of your language, "allow tagging" and "post anonymously by default" settings.
  • Posts: the text posted, whether it is anonymous, its anonymous alias, language and the people tagged; when a post is edited, the text before and after the edit; when it is deleted, the text before deletion.
  • Comments: the text, the post, whether it is anonymous, its alias, whether you are the post's author, and whether the server made the comment anonymous or named for you (see section 4); when a comment is edited, the text before and after; when it is deleted by you or removed by the post owner, the text before that; turning comments on a post off or on; and being refused because of too many comments in a short time.
  • Liking and unliking (posts and comments), reports (reason and note), approving and declining tags, blocking and unblocking, and marking notifications as read. Repeated requests that change nothing (e.g. liking the same post a second time) are not recorded.
  • Moderation: a moderator hiding or restoring a post or a comment.
  • Tags: approving, declining and withdrawing a tag.
  • Account deletion: when you request it, your email address, handle, language, account role, and account creation and age-confirmation dates, and the date the account will be deleted; when you restore it within 30 days, the restore; when the deletion is completed, how much data was erased (no personal data).
  • Legal exports: every export of these records by authorised personnel is itself recorded, together with the query used.

We also store the source port because mobile carriers share one IP address between many users at the same time; the IP address alone may not be enough to match an action to the right person. These records are kept only for write actions; viewing the feed or reading posts is not recorded in them.

Our hosting infrastructure also keeps reverse proxy access logs (IP address, source port, User-Agent and time) for 2 years. These logs are used only for security, troubleshooting and responding to lawful requests.

"Anonymous" means anonymous to other users, not to WishSay or to the authorities. We always store who wrote each post. This lets moderators act on harmful content without seeing who wrote it (e.g. by suspending the account) and lets us respond to lawful requests from authorities. Moderators do not see the author of anonymous content; only authorised personnel with the "legal" role can make that link, on a lawful request. If an authority such as a court or public prosecutor makes a lawful request, the account that wrote an anonymous post can be disclosed to it, together with that account's email address and handle and the IP address, source port and time at which the post was created and edited. The same applies to anonymous comments. Even if the account has been deleted, this link can be made for as long as the activity records are kept (see sections 6 and 8).

Comment aliases. On each post, an anonymous commenter is shown with an alias such as "Anonim #4C6". The alias is computed from the post and your account with a secret key that only WishSay has, so it stays the same for all your anonymous comments on that post, but it is different on every other post: other users cannot tell that two aliases on two posts belong to the same person. If you are the author of an anonymous post, your comments on that post always appear under the post's own alias with an "author" badge, never under your handle, even if you ask for a named comment; the author of a named post always comments under their handle. Comments that you mark as named show your handle. If you comment named and anonymously on the same post, other people may be able to guess from what you write that both are yours.

Who can access activity and consent records? In the app, exporting these records is available only to authorised personnel who have been given a dedicated "legal" role; no other user, including moderators, can access them. Every export is itself written to the activity records, with who ran it and the query used. We disclose these records to authorities only on a lawful request. We assess a request only when we receive it in writing from a competent authority (a court, a public prosecutor or law enforcement); we verify that it comes from that authority and what it covers, and we refuse it if it is not lawful. Every response we give, and which records we shared, is written to the activity records. If someone's life or safety is in serious and imminent danger, we may inform the relevant emergency service without waiting for a request; our legal officer approves that disclosure and it is written to the audit records.

People with technical access to our systems (for example administrators of the database) can access stored data when necessary to run, secure or fix the service. Such access is limited to authorised technical staff: everyone signs in with their own account and key-based authentication, shared passwords are not used, and server and database passwords are kept only as secrets (environment variables), never in code or in the source repository. Accesses are logged.

We may share data with courts, public prosecutors, law enforcement or other competent authorities when the law requires it, or when there is a serious risk to someone's life or safety.

8. Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in the app. Deletion happens in two steps.

Right away, when you ask:

  • All of your sessions are ended immediately on all devices.
  • Your posts (with their images) and comments disappear for everyone, and your likes, tags and the notifications others received about you are no longer shown. Where others replied to one of your comments, an empty "[deleted]" placeholder remains so the replies still make sense.
  • Tag requests waiting for your approval are declined.
  • Your @handle is hidden where others mention it in posts, outcome notes and comments (shown as "@[etiket kaldırıldı]") and cannot be found through search.
  • Your email address and handle are kept for 30 days, so nobody else can register them meanwhile.

Within 30 days you can change your mind: log in with the same email address and password and choose to restore your account. Everything comes back as it was.

After 30 days the deletion is completed automatically:

  • The email address and handle in your account record are replaced with meaningless values, so they no longer identify you. Both become free for someone else to register. (The originals in the activity and consent records are not deleted; see below.) Mentions of your @handle in texts written before the deletion stay hidden; for that, your former handle is kept separately. If someone else registers the handle later, the old mentions are not shown as theirs.
  • Your password is replaced; the account can no longer be logged into or restored.
  • The text of your posts and comments is erased. The files of all images you uploaded are deleted from storage and their descriptions are erased (image fingerprints and storage keys stay for the period in section 6).
  • Your likes, tags (both the ones you are in and the ones on your posts), notifications, blocks (people you blocked and people who blocked you) and session records are deleted.
  • Reports you filed are kept as moderation records, but no longer linked to you.

What is kept: your account record remains with an internal ID, the anonymised email and handle, your language and settings, the age-confirmation and account dates, and the dates of the deletion request and of its completion; your posts and comments remain only as empty placeholders. They no longer identify you, so they are kept without a time limit.

Activity and consent records are not deleted when you delete your account. Because of our legal obligations, the records of actions taken with your account (see section 2) and your consent records are kept after deletion: activity records for 730 days (2 years) from the moment each record was created, and consent records for the same period from the date the deletion was completed. These records still identify you: they contain the email address and handle your account had at registration and at the moment of deletion, the text of your posts and comments (including before and after edits), IP addresses, source ports, device information and times. So although the information in your account record is anonymised, for the duration of the retention period your account and your posts can be linked to you on a lawful request from the authorities. Only the authorised personnel described in section 4 can access these records, and they are deleted automatically when the period ends.

If you want any of the other data above erased as well, contact info@velzon.tr. Activity and consent records, however, cannot be erased before their retention period ends, because of our legal obligation.

10. Security

What we currently do:

  • Passwords are stored only as BCrypt hashes, never in plain text.
  • Access tokens expire after 15 minutes. Refresh tokens expire after 30 days, can be used only once, and are stored only as SHA-256 hashes.
  • If an already-used refresh token is presented again, all of that user's sessions are ended, to protect against stolen sessions.
  • When an account is deleted, its existing tokens stop working immediately.
  • Posts and comments are checked by an automatic word filter, comments are rate-limited (at most 30 in 10 minutes per account), and posts, reports, likes, celebrations, tag requests and searches have per-account limits too (the search counters live only in the server's memory and are not stored). Only reports from accounts that are at least 24 hours old count towards automatically hiding a post. Moderator tools are restricted to moderator accounts.
  • Activity records and consent records are append-only: the database rejects any change to or deletion of these records (the only exception is the automatic job that deletes records whose retention period has ended). Activity records are also chained together: each record carries a hash (SHA-256) that includes the hash of the previous record. This does not make tampering impossible, but if a record is later changed or removed from the middle of the chain, this can be detected. The chain is re-verified, from the oldest stored record to the newest, on every legal export. The newest link of the chain is also written daily to a file kept outside the database, which can only be signed with a secret key held on the server. So even somebody with full access to the database who rewrites the past and recomputes the chain is exposed by the signed record in that file. Consent records are not part of this chain; only the ban on changes and deletions applies to them.
  • Exporting activity and consent records is available only to accounts with a dedicated "legal" role; moderators cannot access these records.

Data sent between the app and our servers is encrypted with TLS (HTTPS). Backups are kept encrypted and are deleted within a 30-day backup cycle. Server access is limited to authorised personnel using SSH keys, and access is logged.

No system is completely secure. If a breach affects your data, we will notify you and the authorities as required by law.

WishSay Content Policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

WishSay is a place to share short "expectations": what you hope for, look forward to, or expect from life, the world or yourself. Posts appear in a shared feed. To keep the feed a good place for everyone, including teens, please follow these rules.

1. What's welcome

  • Hopes, wishes and expectations: "I expect to pass my exams", "I hope this summer is calmer".
  • Honest feelings, including sad or frustrated ones, as long as they don't target or hurt someone.
  • Kind, funny or thoughtful posts in Turkish or English.
  • Tagging a friend in a post they've agreed to (see section 3).

2. What's not allowed

  • Harassment and bullying: insulting, mocking, humiliating or ganging up on someone.
  • Threats: threatening to hurt anyone, or wishing that harm comes to a specific person.
  • Hate speech: attacking people for their ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or similar characteristics.
  • Sexual content: sexually explicit or suggestive posts.
  • Anything that sexualises minors. Zero tolerance. We remove it and may report it to the authorities.
  • Doxxing and personal information: sharing someone's real full name, address, phone number, school, workplace, social media accounts or other private details, including your own.
  • Other people's sensitive information: writing about someone else's health, religion or belief, sex life or similar sensitive matters in a way that can identify them.
  • Encouraging self-harm or suicide: promoting, instructing or egging someone on. Talking about how you feel is okay; see section 8 for help.
  • Violence: glorifying, promoting or describing violence in a way that celebrates it.
  • Spam: ads, repeated posts, links to sell things, or flooding the feed.
  • Impersonation: pretending to be another person, brand or WishSay staff.
  • Pressuring or shaming a real person: using an expectation to put pressure on, guilt, or publicly shame someone ("I expect @x to apologise to me in front of everyone", "I expect [name] to finally admit she lied"). Expectations are about your hopes, not a tool to pressure other people.

Images. You can add up to 4 images to a post (comments cannot have images). All of the rules above apply to images too. In addition, the following are not allowed:

  • Nudity and sexual content: naked or partly naked bodies, sexually suggestive poses or pictures, including drawings, AI-generated and "artistic" ones.
  • Violence and gore: injuries, blood, torture, dead bodies or cruelty to animals; images that glorify violence.
  • Other people's photos without their consent: a photo in which another person's face or identity can be made out, unless they clearly agreed to it being shared. Don't share photos of children.
  • Personal documents: ID cards, passports, driving licences, bank cards, bills, prescriptions, medical reports, screenshots of private chats, or any picture in which an address, phone number, number plate or similar personal information can be read; your own documents included.
  • Content that isn't yours to share: photos, logos or works you have no rights to.

Every uploaded image is automatically screened for nudity and sexual content by an AI model running on our server. An image that looks suspicious is only visible to you until a moderator reviews it; your post is still published. Moderators reject and delete unsuitable images and you are notified. Hidden metadata such as location and camera details is removed from every image automatically, but what is visible in the picture is not.

These rules apply to posts and to comments and replies alike. Posting or commenting anonymously does not change them. You are anonymous only to other users: WishSay keeps the author of every post and every comment and can disclose it to authorities acting on a lawful request. Moderators do not see who wrote anonymous content; where needed they apply sanctions without knowing who it is.

Sensitive information about yourself. Think twice before sharing your own health, religion or belief, sex life or similar sensitive matters: every user can see posts, and a named post shows your handle. The app shows a short reminder when you write a post or a comment. If a post or comment contains your personal or sensitive information, you can ask us to remove it: report it with the reason "Contains my personal / sensitive information", or write to info@velzon.tr.

Explicit consent for sensitive information. To share this kind of information about yourself, you need to give your explicit consent that it will be visible to other users. You give it with a separate checkbox when you sign up. Without it you cannot write new posts or comments or edit existing ones; the app asks you for it. You can withdraw it or give it again at any time under Settings > Sensitive data consent. Withdrawing does not remove what you have already shared; you can delete it or write to us. How the consent is recorded is explained in the Privacy Policy.

3. Tagging etiquette

  • You can only tag people who have turned on "allow tagging" in their settings.
  • A tagged post is published only after every tagged person approves it. If anyone declines, the post isn't published. You won't be told who declined.
  • Anonymous posts can't tag anyone. If you want to mention someone, you have to post under your handle.
  • Only tag people who would be happy to see the post. Don't use tagging to pressure, embarrass or call someone out.
  • Don't send the same tag request over and over to someone who declined.
  • If you don't want to be tagged at all, keep "allow tagging" off (it's off by default).
  • You can change your mind. You can withdraw a tag you approved at any time ("Remove my tag"). The tag is removed, your handle in the post is shown as "@[etiket kaldırıldı]" ("tag removed"), and the post itself stays up. The author is told that a tag was withdrawn, and you will not be asked to approve that post again. If you also want the post gone, report it.

4. Comments and replies

  • You can comment on posts you can see, and reply to comments. Replies to a reply appear in the same thread, marked with who they answer. A comment can be up to 500 characters.
  • Each comment can be anonymous or show your handle; the default follows your "post anonymously by default" setting. Anonymous commenters appear with an alias such as "Anonim #4C6", which stays the same on that post but is different on other posts.
  • The author of an anonymous post always comments on it anonymously, under the post's alias, with an "author" badge.
  • Keep comments about the post. Don't use them to harass the author or other commenters, to guess or reveal who an anonymous author is, or to continue a conversation with someone who blocked you.
  • Post owners can moderate their own posts: they can remove anyone's comment on their post and can turn comments off (and on again) at any time. While comments are off, no new comments or edits can be added, and existing comments stay visible. Removed comments show as "[removed]" if someone had replied to them.
  • You can edit or delete your own comments. Deleted comments show as "[deleted]" if someone had replied to them.
  • To prevent spam, you can write at most 30 comments in 10 minutes.

5. Reporting

  • Tap report on any post or comment and choose a reason: spam, harassment, hate, sexual content, self-harm, violence, "contains my personal / sensitive information" or other. You can add a short note. Reports with the personal / sensitive information reason are flagged for moderators to handle first.
  • The person you report is not told who reported them.
  • When 3 different people report the same post or comment, it is hidden automatically until a moderator reviews it. Only reports from accounts that are at least 24 hours old count towards this number; reports from newer accounts still reach the moderators.
  • Moderators review reports and decide whether the post or comment stays hidden or comes back. A report on a post also covers its images: moderators can remove an image and keep the post.
  • You can also block a user. Their posts and comments disappear from your feed and yours disappear from theirs, and you cannot reply to each other's comments.
  • If you "block the author" of an anonymous post, only that person's anonymous posts and comments are hidden from you. Their profile, named posts and place in search do not change, and they do not notice anything, so the block does not reveal who the author is.

Please don't report posts or comments just because you disagree with them. False or mass reports are themselves a violation.

6. What happens if the rules are broken

  • Automatic filter: some words are blocked automatically, and a post or comment containing them can't be published.
  • Hiding: a post or comment can be hidden by reports or by a moderator. Hidden content is no longer shown.
  • Removal by the post owner: the owner of a post can remove comments on it (see section 4).
  • Account action: for serious or repeated violations we first hide the content and remind you of the rules. If it happens again, your account is suspended for 7 days; the notification states the reason and when the suspension ends. If you carry on after that, or the violation is serious (threats, hate speech, harassment, sexual content), your account is closed permanently.
  • Legal reports: where the law requires it, or where someone may be in danger, we may share information with the competent authorities. See the Privacy Policy.

7. Appeals

If you think your post or comment was hidden by mistake, or you disagree with an action on your account, write to info@velzon.tr with your handle and a short explanation. A moderator will look at it again. A post or comment that is restored after review won't be hidden again by the same reports. A comment removed by the post owner is the owner's decision about their own post and is not reviewed by moderators unless it breaks these rules. We reply to appeals within 7 days at the latest.

8. If you're struggling

If you're thinking about hurting yourself, or someone else is in danger, please don't stay alone with it:

  • If anyone is in immediate danger, call your local emergency services (in Turkey, the emergency number is 112).
  • Talk to someone you trust: a family member, friend, teacher or school counsellor.
  • If you're a university student, you can go to your campus counselling service.
  • Outside Turkey, you can find verified helplines in your country at findahelpline.com.

WishSay is not a crisis service, and moderators can't provide emergency help.

We may update this policy. If we make important changes, we'll let you know in the app.
